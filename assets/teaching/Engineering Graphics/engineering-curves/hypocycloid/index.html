Study Notes: Hypocycloid

Definition: A hypocycloid is the locus of a point on the circumference of a circle when the circle rolls without slipping inside another fixed circle.

1. Introduction

A hypocycloid is an important engineering curve generated by a point attached to the circumference of a generating circle. The generating circle rolls internally, without slipping, along the inside of a directing circle.

The graphical construction is obtained by locating the successive positions of the generating circle and then finding the corresponding positions of the generating point.

2. Generation of a Hypocycloid

Consider a generating circle rolling inside a fixed directing circle. As the generating circle moves, its centre travels along a circular path about the centre of the directing circle. A point fixed on the circumference of the generating circle traces the hypocycloidal locus.

Since the generating circle rolls internally, the centre of the generating circle remains at a distance equal to R − r from the centre of the directing circle.

Radius of centre path = R − r

3. Important Terminology

Directing Circle

The fixed circle inside which the generating circle rolls without slipping.

Generating Circle

The smaller circle which rolls internally inside the directing circle.

Generating Point

The selected point on the circumference of the generating circle whose locus produces the hypocycloid.

Centre of the Directing Circle

The fixed centre about which the centre of the generating circle moves.

Successive Positions

The different positions occupied by the generating circle as it rolls through the specified angular interval.

4. Geometrical Relationship

Let R be the radius of the directing circle and r be the radius of the generating circle. For internal rolling, the centre of the generating circle describes a circle of radius:

OC = R − r

The rolling condition is maintained by making the distance travelled along the directing circle equal to the corresponding arc length travelled around the generating circle.

5. Graphical Construction of a Hypocycloid

  1. Draw the directing circle with centre O.
  2. Mark the required angular interval and divide it into 12 equal parts.
  3. Draw the centre-path circle with radius R − r.
  4. Locate the successive centres of the generating circle on this centre path.
  5. At each centre, construct the corresponding position of the generating circle.
  6. Transfer the corresponding generating-point positions according to the rolling condition.
  7. Mark the generated points successively as the rolling circle moves.
  8. Join the obtained points smoothly to form the hypocycloidal locus.

6. Construction Principle

The essential idea is to combine equal angular divisions of the required movement with the corresponding rolling positions of the generating circle. For every position of the centre, the corresponding position of the generating point is determined geometrically.

Quantity Meaning
R Radius of the directing circle
r Radius of the generating circle
R − r Radius of the path followed by the generating-circle centre
Generating point Point whose locus forms the hypocycloid
12 divisions Equal angular divisions used in the construction

7. Key Points to Remember

  • The generating circle rolls inside the directing circle.
  • The rolling takes place without slipping.
  • The centre of the generating circle moves on a circle of radius R − r.
  • The generating point lies on the circumference of the generating circle.
  • Equal divisions help locate the successive positions accurately.
  • The final hypocycloid should be drawn smoothly through the generated points.

Practice

Practice Questions

Use these questions after studying the notes, presentation and animation.

1. What is a hypocycloid?

A hypocycloid is the locus of a point on the circumference of a circle rolling without slipping inside a fixed directing circle.

2. What is the difference between a hypocycloid and an epicycloid?

In a hypocycloid the generating circle rolls internally inside the directing circle, whereas in an epicycloid it rolls externally.

3. On what path does the centre of the generating circle move?

The centre moves on a circle of radius R − r about the centre of the directing circle.

4. Explain the graphical construction of a hypocycloid.

Divide the required angular interval, locate the successive centres of the internally rolling circle, determine the corresponding generating-point positions and join them smoothly.

5. Why are equal divisions used in the construction?

Equal divisions provide the successive angular positions needed to locate the rolling circle and its generating point accurately.
