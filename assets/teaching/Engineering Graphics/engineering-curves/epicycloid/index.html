Study Notes: Epicycloid

Definition: An epicycloid is the locus of a point on the circumference of a circle when the circle rolls externally, without slipping, on the circumference of another fixed circle.

1. Introduction

An epicycloid is an important engineering curve generated by a point on the circumference of a circle that rolls externally around a fixed directing circle without slipping.

The graphical construction is based on locating successive positions of the generating circle and the corresponding positions of the generating point.

2. Generation of an Epicycloid

Consider a generating circle rolling externally on a fixed directing circle. A point on the circumference of the generating circle is selected as the generating point. As the generating circle moves around the directing circle without slipping, the selected point traces the epicycloidal curve.

The centre of the generating circle moves on a circular path whose radius is the sum of the directing-circle radius and generating-circle radius.

3. Important Terminology

Directing Circle

The fixed circle on whose exterior circumference the generating circle rolls.

Generating Circle

The circle that rolls externally around the directing circle without slipping.

Generating Point

The selected point on the circumference of the generating circle whose locus forms the epicycloid.

Centre of Directing Circle

The fixed centre about which the generating circle rolls.

Centre of Generating Circle

The moving centre of the rolling circle.

Rolling Without Slipping

The contact condition in which the distance travelled along the directing circle corresponds to the rotation of the generating circle.

4. Geometrical Relationship

If the directing-circle radius is R and the generating-circle radius is r, the centre of the generating circle moves on a path of radius:

OC = R + r

For the construction problem used in the presentation:

Directing circle diameter = 90 mm   →   R = 45 mm
Generating circle diameter = 30 mm   →   r = 15 mm
Centre-path radius = R + r = 60 mm

The specified angular travel is 120°, divided into 12 equal parts. Hence each angular division is:

120° / 12 = 10°

5. Graphical Construction of an Epicycloid

  1. Draw the directing circle of the specified diameter with centre O.
  2. Draw the generating circle externally tangent to the directing circle at the starting position.
  3. Mark the generating point on the circumference of the generating circle.
  4. Mark the required angular travel of 120° on the directing circle.
  5. Divide the 120° angular travel into 12 equal parts.
  6. Draw the successive radii from the centre of the directing circle to locate the corresponding positions of the generating-circle centre.
  7. For each centre position, locate the corresponding position of the generating point using the rolling-without-slipping condition.
  8. Mark the successive generated points and join them smoothly to obtain the epicycloid.
  9. Retain the required construction lines and auxiliary elements for graphical verification.

6. Construction Principle

The essential idea is to relate the angular movement of the generating-circle centre around the directing circle to the rotation of the generating circle caused by rolling without slipping. Corresponding positions of the generating point are then obtained and joined smoothly.

7. Key Points to Remember

  • The generating circle rolls externally on the directing circle.
  • The rolling occurs without slipping.
  • The generating point lies on the circumference of the generating circle.
  • The centre of the generating circle moves on a path of radius R + r.
  • Equal angular divisions help locate successive centre positions.
  • The final epicycloid is obtained by joining the generated points smoothly.
  • Construction elements should be retained where required for graphical verification.

Practice

Practice Questions

Use these questions after studying the notes, presentation and animation.

1. What is an epicycloid?

An epicycloid is the locus of a point on the circumference of a circle rolling externally without slipping on a fixed directing circle.

2. What is meant by the directing circle?

It is the fixed circle around which the generating circle rolls externally.

3. What is the generating circle?

It is the circle that rolls externally around the directing circle and generates the curve.

4. Explain rolling without slipping in an epicycloid.

The circumferential travel at the contact corresponds to the rotation of the generating circle, so there is no relative slip at the contact.

5. How is the path of the generating-circle centre determined?

If the directing-circle radius is R and generating-circle radius is r, the centre follows a circular path of radius R + r.

6. Construct an epicycloid for a generating circle of diameter 30 mm rolling externally on a directing circle of diameter 90 mm through 120° divided into 12 equal parts.

Follow the graphical construction procedure given in the Study Notes and the corresponding lecture presentation.

7. What is the angular interval for 12 equal divisions over 120°?

The angular interval is 120° / 12 = 10°.

8. Explain how the successive positions of the generating point are obtained.

Locate each successive centre of the generating circle and determine the corresponding rotated position of the generating point using the no-slip rolling condition.
