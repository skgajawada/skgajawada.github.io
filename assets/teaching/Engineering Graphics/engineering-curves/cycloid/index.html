Study Notes: Cycloid

Definition: A cycloid is the locus of a point on the circumference of a circle when the circle rolls without slipping along a straight directing line.

1. Introduction

A cycloid is one of the important engineering curves studied in Engineering Graphics. It is generated by a fixed point on the circumference of a circle as the circle rolls along a straight line without slipping.

The graphical construction of the curve is based on locating the successive positions of the rolling circle and the corresponding positions of the generating point.

2. Generation of a Cycloid

Consider a circle rolling on a straight directing line. A point marked on the circumference of the circle is initially in contact with the directing line. As the circle rolls, the marked point moves upward, reaches a maximum height, and then returns to the directing line after one complete revolution.

The path traced by this point is called a cycloid. During one complete revolution, the circle travels a distance equal to its circumference.

Distance travelled in one revolution = Circumference of generating circle

3. Important Terminology

Generating Circle

The circle which rolls along the directing line and produces the cycloidal curve.

Generating Point

The selected point on the circumference of the generating circle. The locus of this point forms the cycloid.

Directing Line

The straight line along which the generating circle rolls without slipping.

One Revolution

The portion of the cycloid generated while the circle completes one complete revolution.

4. Geometrical Relationship

If the diameter of the generating circle is D, its circumference is:

C = πD

Therefore, for one complete revolution, the pitch length measured along the directing line is equal to πD.

5. Graphical Construction of a Cycloid

  1. Draw the horizontal directing line on which the generating circle will roll.
  2. Draw the generating circle with the specified diameter and mark the generating point.
  3. Set off a distance equal to the circumference of the circle along the directing line for one complete revolution.
  4. Divide the generating circle into a suitable number of equal parts.
  5. Divide the corresponding pitch distance on the directing line into the same number of equal parts.
  6. Locate the successive centres of the rolling circle.
  7. From the successive centre positions, obtain the corresponding positions of the generating point.
  8. Join the obtained points smoothly and continuously to obtain the cycloidal curve.

6. Construction Principle

The essential idea is to combine two equal divisions: divisions of the generating circle and equal travel intervals of the circle along the directing line. The corresponding point positions are then transferred using the geometry of the rolling circle.

Quantity Meaning
D Diameter of the generating circle
πD Distance travelled during one revolution
Generating point Point whose locus forms the cycloid
Directing line Line along which the circle rolls

7. Key Points to Remember

  • The generating circle rolls without slipping.
  • The generating point lies on the circumference.
  • One revolution produces a cycloidal arch.
  • The travel distance for one revolution is πD.
  • Accurate equal division is important for graphical construction.
  • The final curve should be drawn smoothly through the obtained points.

Practice Questions

Use these questions after studying the notes, presentation and animation.

1. What is a cycloid?

A cycloid is the locus of a point on the circumference of a circle rolling without slipping along a straight directing line.

2. What is meant by the generating circle?

It is the circle that rolls along the directing line and generates the cycloidal curve.

3. What is the distance travelled by the circle in one complete revolution?

It is equal to the circumference of the generating circle, πD, where D is its diameter.

4. Explain the graphical construction procedure for one cycloidal arch.

Draw and divide the generating circle, divide the corresponding pitch distance, locate successive centres and transfer the generating-point positions before drawing a smooth curve through the points.

5. Construct a cycloid for a generating circle of a specified diameter.
