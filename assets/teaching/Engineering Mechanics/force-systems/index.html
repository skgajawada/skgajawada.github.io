Fundamentals

What is a Force System?

Definition

A force system is a collection of two or more forces acting simultaneously on a particle or rigid body.

The effect of the complete force system can often be represented by an equivalent simpler system, such as a single resultant force or a resultant force together with a moment.

R = Σ F
Resultant force is the vector sum of all applied forces.

Physical Interpretation

Instead of analyzing every individual force separately, several forces may be replaced by an equivalent resultant force that produces the same translational effect on the body.

Classification

Types of Force Systems

Force systems can be classified according to the relationship between their lines of action.

Collinear Force System

All forces act along the same straight line. The resultant can be determined algebraically by assigning appropriate signs to the forces.

R = ΣF

Concurrent Force System

The lines of action of all forces intersect at a common point. Vector addition can be used to determine the resultant.

Parallel Force System

The lines of action of the forces are parallel. Their resultant depends on both force magnitude and location.

General Force System

Forces may have arbitrary directions and locations. The equivalent system generally consists of a resultant force and a resultant moment.

Coplanar Force System

All forces act within a single plane. Two-dimensional force-system analysis is commonly used for many engineering structures.

Non-Coplanar Force System

Forces act in three-dimensional space and may have components along all three coordinate directions.

Mathematical Representation

Force as a Vector

Two-Dimensional Force

A force acting in the x-y plane can be resolved into mutually perpendicular components.

Fx = F cos θ

Fy = F sin θ

Therefore,

F = Fx i + Fy j

Three-Dimensional Force

In three-dimensional analysis, the force may be resolved into x, y and z components.

F = Fxi + Fyj + Fzk

The magnitude is

|F| = √(Fx2 + Fy2 + Fz2)
Resultant

Resultant of a Force System

Vector Addition

If several forces act on a body, the resultant force is obtained by vectorially adding all forces.

R = F1 + F2 + F3 + ··· + Fn

Component Form

Rx = ΣFx     Ry = ΣFy
R = √(Rx2 + Ry2)

The direction of the resultant is determined from

θ = tan−1 (Ry / Rx)
Problem Solving

Force-System Analysis Procedure

1

Identify Forces

Identify all externally applied forces and reactions acting on the body.

2

Choose Axes

Establish a suitable coordinate system and reference directions.

3

Resolve Forces

Resolve inclined forces into their Cartesian components.

4

Determine Resultant

Sum the components and calculate the magnitude and direction of the resultant.

Application

Worked Example

Example 1 — Resultant of Two Forces

Two forces of 100 N and 150 N act at angles of 30° and 120°, respectively, measured from the positive x-axis. Determine the resultant force.

Step 1: Resolve the forces into components

F1x = 100 cos 30°
F1y = 100 sin 30°

Step 2: Resolve the second force

F2x = 150 cos 120°
F2y = 150 sin 120°

Step 3: Add the components
Rx = ΣFx
Ry = ΣFy
Step 4: Calculate magnitude
R = √(Rx2 + Ry2)
Step 5: Calculate direction
θ = tan−1 (Ry/Rx)
